About

I am a doctoral researcher in Geospatial Engineering at the Indian Institute of Technology Roorkee, working on high-resolution glacier monitoring in the western Himalaya. My doctoral work investigates high-spatiotemporal glacier change and its driving mechanisms through a combination of terrestrial laser scanning, time-lapse photogrammetry, UAV surveys, and satellite remote sensing.

My research spans ice-cliff backwasting dynamics, debris-covered glacier behaviour, and glacier surface velocity estimation. Alongside the primary glacier research, I have worked on SAR-based terrain classification as part of the NISAR mission testbed, UAV and LiDAR surveys for heritage documentation and impact assessment, and large-scale dumpsite volumetric estimation using drone-derived DSMs.

I completed my Master of Technology in Geo-Informatics with a Gold Medal from MNNIT Allahabad, where my dissertation focused on landslide detection using deep learning. Prior to that I studied Electronics and Communication Engineering at the Birla Institute of Applied Sciences, Nainital. I am an active member of the International Glaciological Society, the American Geophysical Union, and the European Geosciences Union, and have presented my work at international conferences across Asia, Europe, and Central Asia.

Projects
Doctoral Research: High-Spatiotemporal Glacier Change, Western HimalayaJuly 2021 – Present
IIT Roorkee · Supervisor: Prof. Saurabh Vijay

This project centres on understanding how Himalayan glaciers change at fine spatial and temporal scales, and what drives those changes. Using terrestrial laser scanners deployed repeatedly over multiple seasons, high-resolution point clouds capture glacier surface evolution at centimetre precision. Time-lapse cameras installed at glacier margins provide continuous imagery to derive surface displacement and track ice-cliff retreat. The work quantifies backwasting rates of supraglacial ice cliffs at Machoi Glacier and measures ice-flow velocities at Drang Drung Glacier with sub-seasonal temporal resolution. Field campaigns spanning 2022 to 2025 have yielded one of the most temporally dense terrestrial datasets for Himalayan glaciers.

SAR-Based Glacier Terrain Classification for the NISAR Mission2022 – 2024
IIT Roorkee · Collaborative project

As part of preparatory science for the upcoming NASA/ISRO NISAR satellite mission, this project evaluated quad-polarimetric dual-frequency airborne SAR data to map and classify glacierised terrain. The work applied decomposition techniques and machine learning classifiers to distinguish facies such as accumulation zones, debris-covered ice, and periglacial terrain across L- and S-band datasets over Himalayan glaciers.

Endangered Wood Architecture Preservation (EWAP)February 2026 – March 2026
IIT Roorkee · PI: Prof. Ram Sateesh Pasupuleti and Prof. Saurabh Vijay

Two centuries-old wooden temple structures in Uttarkashi were documented using UAV photogrammetry, terrestrial LiDAR scanning, and close-range photography to produce centimetre-accurate 3D models. The geospatial survey was complemented by ethnographic fieldwork including structured interviews with local priests, village heads, and long-term residents to document historical provenance and cultural value.

Heritage Impact Assessment for UNESCO World Heritage SitesJune 2024 – October 2024
Punjab and Haryana High Court, Chandigarh · PI: Prof. Ram Sateesh Pasupuleti and Prof. Saurabh Vijay

UAV surveys generated orthophotos and surface models around Le Corbusier's Capitol Complex and neighbouring protected sites in Chandigarh. Systematic visibility and line-of-sight analyses determined whether proposed constructions would intrude on protected viewsheds, directly informing the Court's heritage conservation decisions.

Dumpsite Garbage Mass Estimation Using UAV PhotogrammetryOctober 2022 – September 2024
Department of Civil Engineering, IIT Roorkee · PI: Prof. Bhanu Prakash Vellanki and Prof. Saurabh Vijay

Fourteen municipal solid waste dumpsite surveys across Uttar Pradesh, Haryana, and Uttarakhand covered sites up to 0.4 square kilometres. Drone-based photogrammetry was combined with depth-wise bulk density sampling to estimate total garbage mass with greater accuracy than conventional area or height-based methods.
